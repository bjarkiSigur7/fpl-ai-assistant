# Chip-Timing Evidence for the Two-Set System (2026-27 Set 1)

**Research date:** 2026-07-23 (launch day +1; GW1 deadline 2026-08-21).
**Question:** Is our model's cascade — **BB GW1 → WC GW2 → TC GW3 (Watkins) → FH GW5**, all four set-1 chips burned by GW5 — defensible, or a known anti-pattern?

**Bottom line (preview):** Each element is *individually* attested somewhere in the community, but **no elite manager, expert panel, or solver-community source endorses burning all four set-1 chips by GW5**. Realized 2025-26 elite behaviour (the first two-set season, structurally identical to 2026-27 set 1: no blanks, no doubles before GW19) clustered chips in **GW5-17**, not GW1-5. Two of our four placements (TC GW3 on a non-premium; FH GW5 for +2.0 xP) contradict both doctrine and realized elite play, and the pattern as a whole is the textbook symptom of our known "greedy within horizon" limitation: chip EV computed only inside GW1-8 assigns **zero opportunity cost** to holding a chip that is actually valid to GW19.

---

## 1. The 2026-27 chip system (CONFIRMED)

- Two sets of Triple Captain, Bench Boost, Free Hit and Wildcard — "once in each half of the season", eight chips total. First set must be used **before the GW19 deadline, 2 Jan 2027, 13:30 GMT**; unused chips are lost and do not roll over. One chip per gameweek. **Free Hit cannot be used in GW1** (and FH in GW19 blocks FH in GW20). ([Premier League: What's happening with FPL chips in 2026/27](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4679879))
- Chips are explicitly **unchanged from 2025-26** — the 2026-27 rule changes are live bonus/ranks, BPS tweaks, later score finalisation, no AFCON transfers this December, and percentage ranks. ([FFS: FPL 2026/27 — 5 rule changes + new features](https://www.fantasyfootballscout.co.uk/2026/07/20/fpl-2026-27-5-rule-changes-new-features-announced)) → **2025-26 set-1 evidence transfers directly.**
- Season runs 22 Aug 2026 – 30 May 2027. The September and October international breaks are **merged into one ~3-week window (21 Sep – 6 Oct)**: the league pauses between GW5 (19 Sep weekend) and GW6 (10 Oct weekend). ([Sky Sports](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11095/13546159/premier-league-26-27-season-start-date-fixture-release-final-day-live-sky-sports-games-and-match-schedule), [This Is Anfield](https://www.thisisanfield.com/2026/03/premier-league-schedule-2026-27-international-break-overhaul/), [AllAboutFPL 2026/27 fixture analysis](https://allaboutfpl.com/2026/07/fpl-fixture-analysis-for-the-2026-27-fpl-season-pl-fixtures/))
- 2025-26 precedent for the structure of the half: **no blank and no double gameweeks existed anywhere in GW1-19** ([FFS: 2025/26 blanks & doubles](https://www.fantasyfootballscout.co.uk/2026/03/19/when-are-the-fpl-blank-and-double-gameweeks-in-2025-26)). All set-1 chips were necessarily played on single gameweeks. Expect the same for 2026-27 set 1.

## 2. Realized 2025-26 evidence — the first two-set season

### 2.1 GW1: early Bench Boost was a small minority play

- GW1 2025-26 chip usage (overall): **Bench Boost 4.7%, Triple Captain 2.2%, WC/FH 0%** (unavailable). "Only a small fraction of managers played chips in Gameweek 1." ([Fantasy Football Fix GW1 stats](https://www.fantasyfootballfix.com/blog-index/fpl-gameweek-1-stats/))
- Outcomes were anecdotal and mixed: one widely-shared account BB'd GW1 into a **127-point week** ([footytimes](https://footytimes.com/unveiling-the-gameweek-1-fpl-triumph-a-127-point-bench-boost-masterstroke)); a content-creator BB GW1 returned **+8** ([gethynellis.com](https://www.gethynellis.com/2025/08/fpl-gameweek-1-bench-boost-transfers-and-a-solid-start/)). No source found that quantified the *average* GW1 BB return across the 4.7%.

### 2.2 Where the elite actually played set-1 chips (Top 50 / elite template)

Fantasy Football Fix's post-season analysis of the 2025-26 **Top 50** ([How the Top 50 Used Double Chips](https://www.fantasyfootballfix.com/blog-index/fpl-top-50-tips-chips-25-26/)):

| Chip | Elite set-1 timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **TC** | **GW6** (major spike) and **GW17** | Both on **Haaland** in single GWs: home v promoted Burnley (5-1; 2G+1A+3BP) and v West Ham (3-0; 2G+1A). "A classic premium captaincy fixture: an elite forward, at home, against a promoted side." |
| **FH** | **GW13** (dominant spike, Top 50 and all-time Top 50) | Used **offensively** to attack a fixture wave (triple City v Leeds), not to solve a blank — there were no blanks. |
| **BB** | "**Far more scattered** … across the opening half" | No single consensus week because there were no early DGWs; managers picked their own settled-squad window. |
| **WC** | No single week reported by Fix; pre-season elite plans clustered **GW6-7** | World #1 Tom Dollimore "likely Wildcarding in GW6" (considering BB GW5); Mansfield, Randazzo, Corbidge on WC6; Baker WC7. ([Fix elite chip-strategy plans, Sep 2025](https://www.fantasyfootballfix.com/blog-index/fpl-2025-26-tips-chips-strategy/)) |

Key structural fact: the elite's set-1 chips went into the **GW5-17 band** — after lineups/form stabilised, at chosen single-GW peaks — not GW1-5 and mostly not the GW18-19 cliff.

### 2.3 The GW19 cliff: forced burns were real, and mostly a mass-market failure

GW19 (last chance for set 1) chip usage ([Fix GW19 stats](https://www.fantasyfootballfix.com/blog-index/fpl-gameweek-19-stats/)):

| Chip | Overall | Top 10k |
|---|---|---|
| Bench Boost | **10.0%** | 4.9% |
| Free Hit | 5.6% | 6.1% |
| Triple Captain | 2.9% | 0.9% |
| Wildcard | 1.5% | 6.3% |

- "Chip usage spiked as Gameweek 19 represented the final opportunity to deploy first-half chips before the reset." One in ten of the entire game BB'd in GW19 — a forced burn at a week not chosen on merit. Top-10k managers were far less exposed (they had spent TC/BB earlier at picked spots), though ~5-6% of even the top 10k dumped BB/FH/WC in the final week.
- FFS was still telling readers in mid-December which chips to emergency-schedule into GW18-19 ([FFS, 16 Dec 2025](https://www.fantasyfootballscout.co.uk/2025/12/16/still-got-fpl-chips-left-heres-when-you-could-use-them)) — i.e., a large share of the player base drifted into the cliff.
- Corroborating scale of mass-market chip waste: LiveFPL's chip tracker at end-of-season showed **top-10k cumulative usage ≈ 99.6-100% per chip**, vs **only ~41-48% among all managers** for the second set ([plan.livefpl.net/chips](https://plan.livefpl.net/chips), accessed 2026-07-23). Elite play = use every chip, at a chosen moment; mass market = let chips die.

**Implication for us:** the failure mode the two-set system actually punished in 2025-26 was **holding too long / drifting into GW19**, not "using chips too early". Our model over-corrects to the opposite extreme, which nobody exhibited.

### 2.4 The 2025-26 champion's own verdict

Erik Ibsen, 2025-26 champion ([Premier League interview, Jul 2026](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4672128/fpl-champion-how-to-pick-your-captain-and-maximise-your-chips)):

- **First half: played his Wildcard in GW2 — and regrets the early approach.** "I think the way I did it the second half season is the way I should approach it from now on."
- Second half (his model): WC GW32 (75 pts) → BB DGW33 (**112 pts**) → FH BGW34 (52) → TC DGW36 (92). "Simple."
- FH philosophy: "**more about being risk-averse than being aggressive**" — held as insurance, used to protect the WC/BB structure.

This is the single most on-point realized data point for our WC GW2: the reigning champion did exactly that and publicly counts it as the thing to fix.

## 3. Community / expert doctrine for set-1 chips

### 3.1 Bench Boost GW1 — the recurring launch-week debate

The official FPL expert panel debated exactly this at the 2025-26 launch ([PL: Is it a good idea to plan for an early Bench Boost?](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4364931/fpl-experts-is-it-a-good-idea-to-bench-boost-in-gameweek-1)). Score: roughly **1 clearly for, 6 against/later, 3 conditional**:

- **For (Nick Harris):** get it out of the way; afterwards "you don't need to worry about maintaining a strong bench … and can start disinvesting" into the XI.
- **Against/later:** Sam Bonfield (prefers **GW8** fixture swings), Utkarsh Dalmia ("a bit overrated", moved to GW8), Lee Bonfield ("least likely to bring a big return"), Holly Shand (abandoned GW1 over enabler fitness; open to GW2-3), Andy North and Az Phillips (**GW16-19**, after AFCON transfers and team-value growth). Conditionals (Buttice, Pras, Sheth) keyed on whether cheap enablers were confirmed starters.
- FFS's dedicated launch article ([GW1 Bench Boost: pros, cons](https://www.fantasyfootballscout.co.uk/2025/08/04/gameweek-1-bench-boost-is-it-a-good-idea/)) lists the same trade-offs and lands neutral. Core cons: **"Gameweek 1 is arguably the most challenging to predict"** (lineup risk on the exact players a BB needs — the £4.5m bench), more information and budget exist later, and the build distorts squad balance.
- The strongest published pro case ([Full90 FPL: Why you should BB GW1](https://full90fpl.com/bench-boost-gameweek-1-fpl/)) argues from the two-set structure: no DGWs in the first half, so a BB is worth "**8-10 points**" whenever you play it; GW1 is the only week the whole 15 is built free (pre-season = de facto wildcard+BB combo); delaying costs transfers to rebuild a boostable bench. It concedes the play requires "mega nailed" enablers.

**Status: BB GW1 is a defensible, contested minority play (4.7% adoption in 2025-26), not a consensus and not a proven trap.** Note our model's +11.1 xP estimate is *above* the community's 8-10pt estimate for a purpose-built GW1 BB — worth stress-testing, since it prices the four cheapest squad slots with zero lineup risk in the single hardest GW to predict.

### 3.2 Early Wildcard (GW2-4) vs holding for the break

- Classic pro-early argument (fix launch mistakes, catch price rises) exists but is the explicitly named **"common mistake"** in launch-week 2026-27 guidance: "burning it early to chase one or two transfers you could have made normally" ([FPL Pilot chip strategy 2026/27](https://www.fplpilot.com/blog/fpl-chip-strategy-2026-27)). [FPL360](https://fpl360.com/2026/05/26/fpl-wildcard-timing-when-to-play-your-cards/) similarly: hold until "a single move cannot fix the squad".
- 2025-26 doctrine put WC1 at **GW7-8** ("by then we'll have seven weeks of data — enough to judge who's real"), fallback GW13 pre-AFCON ([AllAboutFPL first-half chip guide, Sep 2025](https://allaboutfpl.com/2025/09/2025-26-fpl-chip-strategy-guide-first-half-of-the-season/)); elite pre-planning clustered WC **GW6-7** (§2.2); the champion's WC2 is his stated regret (§2.4).
- 2026-27-specific: the **merged 3-week break between GW5 and GW6 (21 Sep – 6 Oct)** is already being flagged as the natural first-wildcard window — time to reassess with 5 GWs of data, and the fixture landscape turns (e.g. Liverpool's first hard fixture, at Man City, is immediately after the break) ([AllAboutFPL fixture analysis](https://allaboutfpl.com/2026/07/fpl-fixture-analysis-for-the-2026-27-fpl-season-pl-fixtures/)).
- **Status: WC GW2 is a known anti-pattern in doctrine** (though the mildest of our four issues, since an early WC does harvest price rises and our plan uses it structurally to fund B. Fernandes). The evidence-backed window is **GW5-8**, ideally over the break. Note also the internal tension: a GW1 squad good enough to Bench Boost should not need tearing up seven days later — if WC2 is right, the GW1 build was wrong.

### 3.3 Triple Captain with no doubles in the half

- With zero DGWs in GW1-19 (confirmed for 2025-26, expected for 2026-27 set 1), the community answer is: **premium captain, at home, against a promoted/bottom side, in a chosen single GW** — precisely what the Top 50 did (Haaland TC GW6 and GW17, §2.2). Even the official launch guidance for 2026-27 says early TC = "premium players facing weaker opponents" ([PL chips article](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4679879)).
- The named 2026-27 candidate windows in early content: **Haaland has two home fixtures against promoted clubs inside the first seven GWs** ([AllAboutFPL fixture analysis](https://allaboutfpl.com/2026/07/fpl-fixture-analysis-for-the-2026-27-fpl-season-pl-fixtures/)).
- **Status: TC GW3 on Watkins (mid-premium) is the clearest anti-pattern in our cascade.** Elite set-1 TC was ~exclusively top-premium (Haaland; secondarily Gabriel-type DGW plays in set 2). Our own plan buys Haaland in GW6 — the doctrinal play is to hold TC for Haaland (8.14 xP GW1-class fixtures recur) rather than spend it in GW3 on a 7-8 xP mid-premium. TC gain ≈ captain's single-GW score, so TC-ing a ~7.6 xP player instead of a later ~8-9 xP Haaland fixture gives up EV *and* the option value of waiting for form/fixture confirmation.

### 3.4 Free Hit in a blank-free half

- Doctrine is unanimous that FH is a **problem-solving / insurance chip**: "Save for awkward weeks … Do not waste it on normal gameweeks with minor gaps" ([FPL Pilot](https://www.fplpilot.com/blog/fpl-chip-strategy-2026-27)); the champion: "more about being risk-averse than being aggressive" (§2.4).
- In the blank-free 2025-26 first half, the realized elite use was **offensive at a picked fixture-wave peak (GW13)** — the chip still waited 13 weeks for its best single-GW spot (§2.2). AllAboutFPL's first-half guide rated FH "least essential" early and slotted it GW6/GW11/GW16-17.
- **Status: FH GW5 for +2.0 xP is an anti-pattern on two counts** — the EV is noise-level (below the ~2-3 pt resolution at which chip plans are distinguishable, §5), and it liquidates the only insurance chip for GWs 6-19 right before a 3-week break that itself provides free replanning time. A +2 in-horizon delta is exactly what a greedy solver produces when it cannot see the GW6-19 alternatives.

## 4. Solver-community practice (sertalpbilal / open-solver / FPL Review ecosystem)

- **Chips are OFF by default in the open solver.** In [sertalpbilal's FPL-Optimization-Tools / open-fpl-solver](https://github.com/solioanalytics/open-fpl-solver), default `chip_limits` = `{"bb": 0, "wc": 0, "fh": 0, "tc": 0}`: the solver never spontaneously spends a chip. Users schedule chips deliberately via `use_wc/use_bb/use_fh/use_tc` (explicit GW lists), or run **scenario sweeps** with `forced_chip_gws` / `allowed_chip_gws` (e.g. `"allowed_chip_gws": {"wc": [25,27], "fh": [30,31]}`) and `iteration_criteria: "chip_gws"` to generate and compare alternative chip plans, with `report_decay_base` for decay-sensitivity ([settings documentation](https://github.com/solioanalytics/open-fpl-solver/blob/main/data/README.md)). The designed workflow is *compare candidate chip weeks across the plausible window*, not *let a short-horizon solve place chips greedily*.
- **Full-window evaluation is the stated methodology where chip optimization is automated.** FPL Copilot's chip optimizer "uses a MIP solver (HiGHS) to evaluate every valid chip combination" and "solves the full transfer and lineup optimization **across your remaining gameweeks**, then ranks by total expected points" ([FPL Copilot chip strategy guide](https://fplcopilot.com/blog/chip-strategy-guide)). I.e., the comparison set for a set-1 chip is GW-now through GW19 — never a truncated horizon.
- No source was found that *explicitly* states "chip horizon must be ≥ chip window" as a rule, but the practice is uniform: forced-chip comparisons span the full candidate window, and the one first-person account of greedy early chip use in the solver community is a cautionary tale — sertalpbilal's own first season: "I had no idea what to do with my chips", forced an early WC by GW6 for a 31-point week, and cites it as what pushed him to build formal chip policies into the model ([AlpsCode: Reflections from my first FPL season](https://alpscode.com/blog/fpl-reflections/)).
- FPL Review's solver docs (docs.fplreview.com) were not fetchable (HTTP 403); its public marketing confirms sandbox/compare-scenario workflow but no quotable horizon rule. **(Gap: no direct FPL Review citation.)**

**Implication:** our method — chip EV via forced-chip re-solves **within GWs 1-8 only** — diverges from documented solver-community practice on exactly the dimension that matters: a chip's alternative use in GW9-19 is priced at zero, so any chip with positive in-horizon delta (even FH's +2.0) gets burned. With decay 0.84, a GW9+ chip week is additionally invisible *and* would be discounted to ~25% by GW9 even if visible.

## 5. Quantified evidence on chip EV and timing

Directly relevant numbers found (all from solver-based, not realized-outcome, analyses — treat as model estimates):

- **Optimal chip timing vs no chips: +49 pts average** across simulated squads (best +73, worst +2) ([FPL Copilot](https://fplcopilot.com/blog/chip-strategy-guide)).
- **Optimal vs random chip timing: 20-30 pts.** Best vs second-best plan: **~3 pts** — chip plans are nearly flat near the optimum, so small in-horizon deltas (our FH +2.0, WC +4.1) are within noise of alternative timings *that the solver never evaluated*.
- **Bench Boost: 8-12 pts in a single GW vs 15-25 in a double** (ibid.); Full90's purpose-built GW1 BB estimate: 8-10 pts. In a DGW-less half, the BB timing surface is flat (Fix: elite BB "far more scattered") — so BB GW1 loses little *if* the bench is truly nailed; its risk is lineup uncertainty, not timing.
- **TC in a single GW ≈ +1× the captain's single-GW score** — so TC placement in a DGW-less half is a pure max-xP-fixture hunt; realized elite spikes were Haaland GW6/GW17 (§2.2), i.e. they waited for the year's best captain fixture rather than taking week 3's.
- **No published study quantifies "all four chips by GW5 vs spread to GW19".** Synthesis from the above (inference, clearly labelled): TC Watkins GW3 vs TC Haaland at a GW6-17 peak ≈ −1 to −3; FH at +2.0 vs a picked GW6-19 wave/insurance use (champion's realized FH: 52 pts on a week his squad was weak) ≈ −2 to −6; BB GW1 vs settled-bench GW5-8 ≈ 0 to −4 plus GW1 lineup risk; WC GW2 vs over-the-break GW5/6 ≈ 0 to −5 in fixture-turn value (partly offset by price-rise capture). **Rough total: −3 to −18 xP vs an evidence-based spread (BB1 *or* BB5-8, WC over the GW5-6 break, TC on Haaland's best home-promoted fixture, FH held as insurance / best wave), before counting variance-insurance value of a held FH.**

## 6. Verdict on our cascade, chip by chip

| Our play | Community status | Evidence quality | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BB GW1** (+11.1) | Contested minority play; 4.7% adoption 2025-26; expert panel mostly against, one notable for; pro case values it 8-10 pts | Strong (realized % + on-record debate) | **Defensible**, but our +11.1 exceeds community estimates; require nailed-bench verification; GW1 is the highest-lineup-risk week of the season |
| **WC GW2** (+4.1) | Doctrine anti-pattern ("common mistake"); champion did it and regrets it; elite window GW5-8; 2026-27 natural slot = the 3-week GW5→GW6 break | Strong (champion testimony + consistent doctrine) | **Weakly defensible at best**; also logically tensioned with BB GW1 (boost-worthy squad shouldn't need a week-2 teardown). +4.1 is near plan-noise (~3 pts) |
| **TC GW3 Watkins** (+7.6) | Anti-pattern: elite set-1 TC was premium-only (Haaland GW6/GW17), on the half's best fixture, not week 3 | Strong (Top-50 realized data) | **Not defensible** — our own plan acquires Haaland GW6; hold TC for his best home-promoted fixture |
| **FH GW5** (+2.0) | Anti-pattern: FH = insurance/problem chip or picked fixture-wave attack (elite: GW13); +2 is below the ~3-pt plan-resolution noise floor | Strong (doctrine + realized elite timing) | **Not defensible** — clearest symptom of the greedy-within-horizon bug |
| **All 4 by GW5** | No precedent found in elite realized play, expert doctrine, or solver practice; elite spread = GW5-17; the system's real punished failure was the *opposite* (GW19 cliff drift, mass-market) | Strong | **Anti-pattern as a package**; estimated cost −3 to −18 xP vs evidence-based spread (§5, inference) |

### What the two-set system *does* justify (steelman for early chips)

- Chips genuinely can't be hoarded past GW19 and the first half has **no DGWs**, so peak weeks are much flatter than in a classic season — the *penalty* for early use is smaller than pre-2025 intuition suggests ([Full90](https://full90fpl.com/bench-boost-gameweek-1-fpl/), [AllAboutFPL](https://allaboutfpl.com/2025/09/2025-26-fpl-chip-strategy-guide-first-half-of-the-season/)).
- The mass-market failure mode was holding too long (10% of the game BB'd in GW19; ~half of all managers let set-2 chips die — [Fix GW19](https://www.fantasyfootballfix.com/blog-index/fpl-gameweek-19-stats/), [LiveFPL](https://plan.livefpl.net/chips)). A model that schedules all chips deliberately inside the window is directionally right.
- But "use them all by GW19 at chosen peaks" ≠ "use them all by GW5". The elite solution was mid-half deployment at hand-picked single-GW peaks.

### Recommended model changes

1. **Extend chip-EV evaluation to the full set-1 window (GW1-19)**, decoupled from the transfer-planning horizon: run forced-chip sweeps per chip per candidate GW over GW1-19 (coarse projections beyond GW8 are fine — fixture-based xP is enough to price opportunity cost), matching documented solver practice ([FPL Copilot methodology](https://fplcopilot.com/blog/chip-strategy-guide), [open-solver `forced_chip_gws`/`allowed_chip_gws` workflow](https://github.com/solioanalytics/open-fpl-solver/blob/main/data/README.md)).
2. Failing that, **add an explicit hold value** per chip ≈ max in-window forced-chip delta beyond the horizon (cheap proxy: TC ≈ best remaining Haaland home-promoted xP; FH ≈ 4-6 pts insurance/wave value; BB ≈ best settled-bench week; WC ≈ fixture-turn value at the GW5-6 break). Only burn in-horizon if the in-horizon delta beats the hold value plus a noise margin (~3 pts).
3. **Constrain TC to top-premium captains** (Haaland-class) unless the delta over the best held alternative exceeds the noise margin.
4. **Treat FH as held-by-default insurance** with a trigger threshold (e.g. only play for delta ≥ ~6 pts or to solve a broken week).
5. Re-check the BB GW1 bench for nailedness (the entire chip's value sits on four ~£4.5-5.0m players in the least predictable GW of the season) and validate +11.1 against the community's 8-10pt prior.
6. Keep the GW19 hard stop as a constraint so no chip can drift past the cliff — the one mistake the mass market actually made.
